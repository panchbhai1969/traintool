## Image-quality gate

First assess whether the relevant enamel surface is visible, adequately focused, and not obscured by glare, saliva, soft tissue, shadow, or another tooth. This is a visual screening judgment from a photograph, not a radiographic diagnosis. Do not infer hidden proximal, pulpal, or subsurface disease.

## Definition

Visible dental caries is a localized disease-related change in tooth structure. Relevant photographic signs include cavitation, surface breakdown, undermined enamel, a rough or irregular destructive defect, or a chalky-white or brown/black lesion whose opacity, texture, and location support demineralization rather than a removable surface deposit.

## Positive evidence

Return a present verdict only when the visible tooth structure supports a carious lesion. Look for one strong structural sign or several concordant visual signs:

- A cavity, pit, hole, broken-down surface, undermined edge, or localized loss of tooth structure.
- Chalky, matte, opaque white enamel consistent with demineralization rather than glare.
- Brown or black discoloration associated with roughness, cavitation, an irregular defect, or destructive contour change.
- A lesion pattern in pits, fissures, cervical areas, or accessible proximal surfaces that is anatomically plausible for caries.
- A clear localized difference from adjacent intact, smooth, normally translucent enamel.

## Exclusions and differentials

Do not label the following as caries:

- Flat extrinsic stain with intact, smooth enamel and no destructive change.
- Dental calculus or plaque sitting on top of the tooth surface.
- Normal pits and fissures without opacity, breakdown, or other disease evidence.
- Attrition, abrasion, erosion, fractured enamel, or exposed dentine from non-carious wear.
- Metallic or tooth-colored restorations, restoration margins, crowns, veneers, or sealants.
- Natural shade variation, cervical dentine show-through, fluorosis, or intrinsic discoloration.
- Interproximal, vestibular, lip, or cheek shadow.
- Specular highlights, blur, compression artifacts, and dark areas created by uneven lighting.

## Adjudication

Structural evidence outweighs color. A dark mark alone is not enough for a present verdict. Compare the suspected region with adjacent enamel and explain whether the surface appears intact or destroyed. When image quality is inadequate or the finding could reasonably be stain, calculus, restoration, wear, or shadow without visible tissue destruction, use an absent verdict and state the limitation.
